## Why this manual exists

The company acts as a customs representative for many of its customers, which means the company puts its own name and its own authorisations behind declarations made on their behalf. An error in a declaration is the company's error, whatever the customer told us, and the cost of getting it wrong ranges from a delayed consignment to the loss of an authorisation the business depends on.

This manual applies to everyone who prepares, checks, submits or amends a customs declaration, and to the commercial staff who agree the terms on which the company does so. It is read alongside the country appendices, which record the local procedures, the authorisation numbers and the contacts at each customs authority.

## Classification

Every item is classified to the correct commodity code before a declaration is made. Classification determines the duty rate, the import controls, the licences required and the statistical reporting, and a plausible-looking code that is wrong is worse than no code, because it passes.

Classification is based on what the goods are, not on what they are used for or what the customer calls them in their own systems. Where the description supplied is insufficient, the declaration is not submitted on a best guess; the missing information is requested. Rulings obtained from a customs authority are recorded in the classification library with their validity dates and are followed for as long as they remain valid. Where the company's classification differs from the customer's instruction, the difference is raised with the customer in writing before submission, and the decision is documented.

## Valuation and origin

Customs value is normally the transaction value, adjusted for the additions and deductions the law prescribes: freight and insurance to the place of introduction, royalties, assists, and so on. Where the parties are related, the transaction value is accepted only where the relationship did not influence the price, and the basis for that conclusion is recorded.

Origin determines preferential duty rates and is not the same as the country the goods were shipped from. Preference is claimed only where a valid proof of origin is held at the time of the claim, not expected to arrive later. Statements on origin, certificates and supplier declarations are checked for validity dates, correct exporter identification and the right legal wording, and are retained with the declaration. A preference claimed without a valid proof exposes the customer to a retrospective demand and the company to a compliance finding.

## Licences, controls and sanctions

Before a declaration is submitted, the item is checked against the control lists applying to the route: dual-use, military, endangered species, precursor chemicals, waste shipments and agricultural controls, among others. Where a licence is required, its number and validity are recorded on the declaration and a copy is held.

Sanctions screening covers the parties to the movement and, where relevant, the vessel and the end use. A match, or a near match that cannot be cleared, stops the movement and is escalated to Compliance the same day; it is not cleared by the person who found it. Consignments with a destination or a party subject to sanctions are not moved on the basis of a customer's assurance, however longstanding the relationship. Compliance maintains the screening tool and the record of every alert raised and how it was resolved.

## Making and amending declarations

Declarations are prepared from the documents, not from memory or from a previous consignment copied forward. Repeating last month's declaration for a similar shipment is the single most common source of error found in the company's internal audits, and templates are therefore locked to the fields that genuinely repeat.

Every declaration is checked by a second person before submission where the duty at stake exceeds 5,000 EUR, where a licence or preference is claimed, or where the commodity code is used for the first time by that customer. Errors found after submission are corrected by a voluntary amendment as soon as they are identified. Voluntary disclosure of an underpayment consistently attracts better treatment from customs authorities than the same error found by them at audit, and the decision to disclose belongs to Compliance rather than to the commercial team.

## Records and audit

Declarations and their supporting documents are retained for the statutory period in the country concerned, which is seven years in the European Union and six in the United Kingdom, measured from the end of the year in which the declaration was accepted. The supporting set includes the commercial invoice, the packing list, the transport document, the proof of origin, any licence and the instruction from the customer.

Records are held in the customs system and not in individual mailboxes. The company's authorisations are subject to periodic audit by the customs authority, which may request any record at short notice. Internal audits are carried out quarterly on a sample of declarations per country, and findings are tracked to closure with named owners. A finding that recurs across two consecutive audits is escalated to the compliance committee.

## Authorisations and what puts them at risk

The company holds authorisations that the business depends on commercially: authorised economic operator status, customs warehousing, inward processing, and simplified declaration procedures in several countries. Each carries conditions on record keeping, financial solvency, practical competence and the integrity of the people involved.

Losing an authorisation is rarely the result of one dramatic failure. It is the result of a pattern: findings not closed, staff not trained, records not retained, amendments not made. Each authorisation has a named owner in Compliance responsible for its conditions, its renewal date and its evidence file. Any event that could affect an authorisation, including a customs finding, an insolvency event, a change of control or the departure of a named competent person, is notified to the authority within the period its conditions require, which is usually far shorter than people expect.
