## How support is organised

The company's customer support operates from Rotterdam and Halifax, covering 05:00 to 23:00 Central European Time on working days and 08:00 to 18:00 at weekends, with an out-of-hours duty controller reachable at all other times for consignments in transit.

Tier one handles tracking enquiries, documentation reissues, booking amendments and the first assessment of anything else. Tier two handles claims, customs holds, temperature excursions and any case where a customer has escalated. The account management team owns the commercial relationship and is informed rather than involved in day-to-day cases. Every case has one owner at any moment, named in the case record, and ownership transfers explicitly rather than by a case sitting untouched in a shared queue.

## Response and resolution targets

First response is within one hour for cases raised by phone, four hours for email during covered hours, and by 09:00 the next working day for anything arriving out of hours. These targets are measured from arrival, not from the point the case reaches the right team.

Resolution targets vary by case type: a tracking enquiry closes the same day, a documentation reissue within one working day, a booking amendment before the next scheduled movement, and a claim within the thirty days set by the claims procedure. Where a target will be missed, the owner contacts the customer before it is missed, with a revised date and the reason. A missed target the customer was warned about damages far less than one they discover themselves.

## Handling a delayed or missing consignment

Confirm the consignment reference and the last confirmed scan before saying anything about its location. Check the movement record, the terminal handover log and the carrier's own tracking, in that order, and record what each one shows with the time you checked it.

Where a consignment has no scan for more than twelve hours against a schedule that expected one, open a trace with the responsible terminal and inform the customer proactively that you have done so. Do not offer a revised delivery date until the trace has produced a location, because a second missed date costs more trust than an honest statement that the location is not yet confirmed. Consignments still unlocated after seventy-two hours are escalated to tier two and treated as a potential loss under the claims procedure.

## Temperature excursions and damaged goods

Any report of a temperature excursion on a controlled consignment is escalated to tier two immediately and to the duty controller if out of hours. The affected product is quarantined and must not be released for delivery while the excursion is under assessment, whatever the customer's commercial urgency and whatever the reading appears to show.

Obtain the temperature record from the storage or transport provider and attach it to the case before contacting the customer. The disposition decision belongs to the consignment owner, not to the company and not to the storage provider, and support's role is to present the record and obtain a written instruction. For damaged goods, obtain photographs at the point of discovery, note the damage on the proof of delivery where the discovery is at delivery, and open a claim record within three working days.

## Claims

A claim is opened when a customer notifies loss, damage or delay causing a quantified cost. Record the consignment reference, the notification date, the amount claimed and the basis for it. The notification date matters because the contractual windows run from it, and support records the date the customer told us rather than the date the case was correctly categorised.

Acknowledge every claim within two working days. Gather the proof of delivery, temperature records where relevant, photographs, the commercial invoice for the goods and the movement history, and pass a complete pack to tier two. Do not admit or deny liability in correspondence with the customer, and do not discuss the limits in the underlying carrier contract; those are commercial matters handled by tier two and Legal. Claims are settled or formally declined within thirty days of a complete pack.

## Escalation

A case escalates when the customer asks for it, when a target has been missed twice, when the value at stake exceeds 10,000 EUR, or when the case involves a regulated product, a safety matter or a potential data breach. Escalation is recorded in the case with the reason and the time.

Tier two acknowledges an escalation within one hour during covered hours. Escalation to the head of customer operations follows where tier two cannot resolve within two working days or where the customer has threatened to terminate. Account management is informed of every escalation on the same day, in writing, whether or not they are asked to act, so that a commercial conversation is never the first they hear of an operational failure.

## Tone, records and what not to do

Write to customers in plain language, in full sentences, using the consignment reference in the first line. State what happened, what is being done, who is doing it and when they will hear next. Avoid the passive constructions that operations writing attracts, because a customer reading that a delay was experienced does not learn who is fixing it.

Every material action and conversation is recorded in the case, including telephone calls, on the day it happens. Do not correspond about a case from personal email or messaging apps. Do not share another customer's information, including by forwarding a thread without checking it. Do not speculate about cause, and never blame a named colleague, a named supplier or a named carrier in writing to a customer.
