## General site rules

Everyone entering a company terminal signs in, wears high-visibility clothing and safety footwear, and follows the marked pedestrian routes. Visitors are escorted at all times and do not enter operational areas without an escort, whatever their seniority and whatever their hurry.

Mobile telephones are not used while walking in a vehicle operating area, and not used at all while operating equipment. Headphones are not worn outside the office areas. Smoking and vaping are permitted only in the designated area outside the gate. Anyone on site who appears impaired by alcohol, drugs or fatigue is removed from operational duties immediately by the shift supervisor, and the matter is handled afterwards; the removal is not a disciplinary act and is not negotiable at the time.

## Vehicle and pedestrian segregation

Vehicles and pedestrians are separated by physical barriers wherever the layout allows and by marked routes everywhere else. Pedestrians cross vehicle lanes only at marked crossings, and make eye contact with the driver before crossing. Where eye contact is not possible, wait.

Reversing is the highest-risk manoeuvre on any terminal. Reversing into a dock is done with a banksman unless the dock is fitted with a working guidance system. The banksman stands where the driver can see them, on the driver's side, and stops the manoeuvre the moment they lose sight of the driver or the driver loses sight of them. Yard tractors and trailers are never moved with anyone inside the trailer or on the dock leveller, and the dock lock or wheel chock is engaged before any loading begins.

## Powered equipment

Forklifts, reach trucks, pallet trucks and order pickers are operated only by people holding current certification for that specific machine type, held on file at the terminal. Certification from a previous employer is verified before it is accepted, and expired certification means the person does not operate, without exception.

Operators complete a documented pre-use check at the start of each shift: forks, chains, tyres, brakes, horn, lights, battery and any hydraulic leak. A defect found is recorded and the machine is tagged out of service until it is repaired, by the operator who found it, immediately, rather than reported for someone else to tag. Keys are removed and forks lowered whenever a machine is left. Nobody rides on a machine not designed to carry a passenger, and nobody is lifted on forks or on a pallet under any circumstances.

## Manual handling and racking

Loads are assessed before they are lifted. Use mechanical aids where they are available, and ask for help where they are not. The guideline limits are 25 kilograms for a lift at waist height close to the body, reducing sharply for lifts above shoulder height, below knee height or away from the body. These are guidelines for planning, not permissions: a load within the limit that cannot be lifted safely in the actual conditions is not lifted.

Racking is loaded within the posted beam and bay limits, with the heaviest loads at the lowest levels. Damaged racking is reported immediately and the affected bay is emptied and cordoned, not simply avoided. Racking inspections are carried out weekly by the terminal manager and annually by a competent external inspector, and the records are kept on site for three years.

## Dangerous goods and spills

Dangerous goods are accepted only with complete and correct documentation and correct labelling, and are stored in the designated segregated area according to the compatibility chart posted there. Where documentation is incomplete, the consignment is not accepted; it is not accepted conditionally pending paperwork.

Spill kits are located at each dangerous goods area and at each dock. On discovering a spill, stop, do not attempt to identify a substance by smell or touch, and alert the shift supervisor. Only people trained in spill response deal with anything beyond a small, identified, non-hazardous spill. Evacuate the immediate area, contain the spread if it is safe to do so, and consult the safety data sheet before selecting an absorbent. All spills, however small, are recorded.

## Fire, evacuation and first aid

Fire exits and the routes to them are kept clear at all times, and pallets are never stacked in front of an exit or under a sprinkler head within the posted clearance. Fire extinguishers are inspected annually and after any use, and their locations are not obstructed.

On hearing the alarm, stop work, make equipment safe where it takes seconds to do so, and leave by the nearest exit to the assembly point. Do not collect belongings and do not re-enter until the fire marshal says so. Each shift has a nominated first aider and fire marshal, named on the board at the entrance. Evacuation drills are held twice a year on every site, at least one of them on a night shift, because a drill that only ever happens in daylight tests only half the operation.

## Reporting, investigation and stopping work

All injuries, near misses and dangerous occurrences are reported to the shift supervisor before the end of the shift and entered in the safety system the same day. Near misses are reported with the same seriousness as injuries; they are the only warning the company gets that costs nothing.

Incidents are investigated to find the conditions that made them possible, not the person at the end of them. Anyone at any level may stop a task they believe to be unsafe, and the task stays stopped until the shift supervisor has assessed it. No one has been or will be disciplined for stopping work in good faith, and a supervisor who discourages it is themselves in breach of this manual.
