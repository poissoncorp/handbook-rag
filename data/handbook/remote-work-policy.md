## Who this policy covers

This policy applies to office-based roles at the company where the work can be performed away from a company site without loss of quality or control. Operations roles tied to a terminal, warehouse or vehicle are excluded by the nature of the work, and no manager may agree a remote arrangement for them.

Eligibility is decided by the role, not by the person. A role is designated remote-eligible by the function head, recorded in the role catalogue, and reviewed when the role changes. An employee in a remote-eligible role may request a hybrid or fully remote arrangement; the request is considered on its merits and may be declined with written reasons. An arrangement agreed under this policy is not a permanent contractual right and may be withdrawn on four weeks' notice where business need requires it.

## Hybrid arrangements

The default arrangement is hybrid: three days on site and two days remote in each full working week. Teams choose which days are on site and publish them, so that time on site is time the team is actually together rather than five people alone in an office on different days.

Anchor days are set at team level, not individually, and are reviewed each quarter. Where a team spans two locations, the anchor days are aligned across both. Employees may swap a remote day for an on-site day freely. Swapping an on-site day for a remote day needs the manager's agreement and should not reduce on-site attendance below two days in any week. Attendance is not tracked by badge data for performance purposes, and managers are asked to treat patterns rather than individual days.

## Fully remote arrangements

A fully remote arrangement is available where the role is designated remote-eligible, the employee has completed probation, and the function head approves. It is documented in a written variation to the contract that records the employee's home working location and the country of that location.

The registered working country matters because it determines tax, social insurance and employment law exposure for the company. An employee must not work from a country other than their registered one for more than ten consecutive working days, or thirty days in a rolling year, without prior approval from People Operations and Finance. This is not a matter of trust: extended presence in another country can create a taxable establishment for the company, and the exposure is the company's rather than the employee's.

## Equipment, expenses and the home workspace

The company provides a laptop, a headset and a second monitor to everyone working remotely on a regular basis. Desks, chairs and lighting are the employee's responsibility, with a one-off allowance of 400 EUR available on request against receipts, renewable every four years.

Home internet is not reimbursed. Mobile data is reimbursed where the role requires it and the employee has no company phone. Employees working remotely are responsible for a workspace that is safe and reasonably free of interruption, and complete a short self-assessment of it annually. Equipment provided for home use remains company property under the same terms as any other issued equipment and is returned on the last working day, arranged and paid for by the company.

## Security expectations when working away from a site

Work is performed on company-issued equipment only. Company data must not be processed on a personal computer, a personal cloud account or a personal email address, whatever the convenience. Screens showing customer data must not be visible to anyone outside the company, which in practice rules out working on customer accounts in cafes, on trains and in shared coworking spaces.

Public wireless networks may be used only with the company VPN active. Home wireless networks must use WPA2 or better and must not use a default administrator password on the router. Devices must remain enrolled in mobile device management and must not have disk encryption disabled. Loss or theft of a device is reported to the service desk immediately, day or night, using the emergency number, and not left until the next working day.

## Meetings and written communication

A meeting with any remote participant is run as a remote meeting: everyone dials in individually, from their own camera and microphone, rather than some people sharing a room and one laptop. This is the single change that most determines whether hybrid works, and managers are expected to hold to it even when it feels unnecessary.

Decisions taken in a meeting are written up in the team's shared workspace within one working day, by the person who called the meeting. Work that depends on availability rather than presence is planned asynchronously by default, with a stated deadline rather than a scheduled call. Core hours of 10:00 to 15:00 in the employee's registered location are protected for collaboration; outside them, colleagues are not expected to respond immediately.

## Reviewing and withdrawing an arrangement

Managers review remote arrangements at each half-year performance review. The review asks whether the work is being delivered, whether the team is functioning, and whether the arrangement still fits the role, and it records the answer.

An arrangement may be withdrawn where performance has declined and the manager can connect the decline to the arrangement, where the role has changed such that it is no longer remote-eligible, or where the employee has repeatedly breached the security expectations in this policy. Withdrawal is confirmed in writing with four weeks' notice and may be appealed to the function head within ten working days. An employee who is asked to return to a site full time and is unable to do so may raise it as a flexible working request under the separate statutory procedure.
