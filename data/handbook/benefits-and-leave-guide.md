## What this guide covers

This guide describes the benefits available to the company employees and the leave they may take beyond annual leave. Entitlements differ by country of employment, and where they do, the country-specific appendix on the intranet governs. Nothing here overrides a statutory entitlement that is more generous.

Benefits are reviewed annually. Where a benefit is provided through an insurer or a scheme administrator, the scheme rules govern the detail, and this guide is a summary of them rather than a substitute. Employees are encouraged to read the scheme documents at least once, particularly for income protection and life assurance, where the definitions do the real work and the summary necessarily flattens them.

## Pension and retirement savings

The company contributes to a defined contribution pension for every employee from the first day of employment, with no waiting period. The employer contribution is six percent of base salary, rising to eight percent after five years of service.

Employees may contribute from zero upward. The company matches employee contributions up to a further three percent, so an employee contributing three percent receives a total employer contribution of nine percent, or eleven percent after five years. Contributions are made through payroll, which in most countries gives tax relief at source. Investment choices are made by the employee through the scheme administrator's portal, and a default lifestyle fund applies to anyone who makes no choice. People Operations cannot give investment advice, and an independent adviser session is offered annually at company cost.

## Health and insurance benefits

Private medical cover is provided for the employee, with the option to add a partner and children at the employee's cost through payroll. Cover includes inpatient and day-patient treatment, outpatient treatment up to an annual limit, and mental health support with no separate limit.

Life assurance is four times base salary, paid to the employee's nominated beneficiary; nominations are made on the workforce portal and should be reviewed after any significant change in circumstances. Income protection pays seventy-five percent of base salary after twenty-six weeks of continuous absence, subject to the insurer's definition of incapacity, and continues until return to work, retirement or the end of the policy term. A confidential employee assistance programme is available to every employee and their household, free, at any hour, and the company receives no information about who uses it.

## Sick leave

Employees unable to work through illness notify their manager as early as possible and in any event before the start of the shift or working day. Notification is by telephone or message to the manager directly, not by leaving it with a colleague.

Company sick pay is full pay for the first twenty-six weeks of absence in a rolling twelve-month period, inclusive of any statutory sick pay, after which income protection may apply. A medical certificate is required from the eighth consecutive calendar day of absence and for every subsequent period. Managers hold a return-to-work conversation after every absence, however short, which is a supportive conversation and not an investigation. Repeated short absences trigger a review of whether an underlying condition needs adjustment rather than a disciplinary response.

## Parental and family leave

Maternity, paternity, adoption and shared parental leave follow the statutory framework in the country of employment, enhanced by the company as follows: the primary carer receives twenty-six weeks at full pay, and the secondary carer receives eight weeks at full pay, both from day one of employment and irrespective of gender or how the family was formed.

Employees returning from a period of parental leave longer than six months may request a phased return of up to eight weeks at reduced hours on full pay. Fertility treatment appointments and antenatal appointments are paid time off, for the employee and for a partner accompanying them. Pregnancy loss at any stage entitles both parents to two weeks of paid leave, and no medical evidence is asked for. Emergency dependant leave of up to five paid days a year is available for the unexpected: a childcare failure, a hospital admission, a care arrangement that collapses.

## Other leave

Bereavement leave is five paid days for the death of a partner, child or parent, and three paid days for another close relative, extendable at the manager's discretion, which managers are expected to use generously rather than sparingly. Employees may take up to two paid days a year for volunteering with a registered charity.

Unpaid leave of up to three months may be requested after two years of service, subject to business need and function head approval; service and benefits continue but pension contributions pause. Jury service and reserve forces duties are paid, with any allowance received from the court or the forces offset against pay. A sabbatical of up to six months, unpaid, may be requested after five years of service and is granted at the company's discretion with the role held open.

## Making a claim or asking a question

Claims under the medical scheme are made directly to the insurer using the membership number on the workforce portal, not through People Operations, which has no access to individual claims and is not told about them.

Questions about entitlement, pay during leave, or how two entitlements interact go to the People Operations mailbox and are answered within three working days. Where a question involves a medical matter, employees may speak to the occupational health provider directly and confidentially, and only the resulting recommendation on fitness for work and adjustments is shared with the manager. Anyone who believes a benefit or leave entitlement has been wrongly refused may raise it under the grievance procedure in the employee handbook.
