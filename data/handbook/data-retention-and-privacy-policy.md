## Purpose and legal basis

This policy sets out how the company collects, uses, stores and disposes of personal data, and how long it keeps records of every kind. It gives effect to the General Data Protection Regulation and the equivalent legislation in each country where the company operates.

The company processes personal data on four bases: performance of a contract, for employee and customer contact data needed to move consignments; legal obligation, for customs, tax and employment records; legitimate interest, for security monitoring and fraud prevention; and consent, for marketing communications only. Consent is never used as the basis for anything the company needs to do anyway, because consent can be withdrawn and processing that must continue cannot depend on it. The lawful basis for each processing activity is recorded in the processing register maintained by Legal.

## Categories of data and who holds them

Employee data is held by People Operations and includes contract, payroll, performance, absence and, where relevant, occupational health records. Access is restricted to People Operations, the individual's own manager for a defined subset, and Payroll.

Customer data is held by Commercial and Customer Operations and includes contact details, consignment details, commercial terms and correspondence. Consignee data, often collected from the customer rather than the individual, includes delivery addresses and telephone numbers and is held for the duration of the movement and the claims window. Supplier data includes contact details of individuals at supplier organisations. Security monitoring data, including access logs, camera footage and endpoint telemetry, is held by IT Security under separate retention rules.

## Retention periods

Employee records are retained for seven years after employment ends, except payroll and tax records, which follow the local statutory period, and occupational health records, which are retained for forty years where required by health and safety law.

Customer and consignment records are retained for seven years from the end of the contract year, which reflects the limitation period for contractual claims. Customs declarations and supporting documents are retained for the period required by the customs authority of the country concerned, which is seven years in the European Union and six in the United Kingdom. Camera footage is retained for thirty-one days unless preserved for an investigation. Access and security logs are retained for thirteen months. Unsuccessful job applications are retained for twelve months. Marketing contact data is deleted three years after the last interaction.

## Deletion and the practice of it

Retention periods are maxima, not targets. Data that is no longer needed is deleted before its period expires, and a system that cannot delete is not a reason to keep data indefinitely; it is a defect to be raised with the system owner.

Deletion means deletion from production systems, from backups as they rotate out, and from any extract taken for analysis. Backups are not exempt: where an individual's data must be erased and it exists in a backup, the backup is not restored selectively but the record is suppressed on any restore, and the backup is allowed to expire on its normal cycle. Each system owner runs a documented deletion job at least annually and records the date it ran and the volume removed. Legal audits a sample of these records each year.

## Rights of individuals

Individuals may ask for a copy of their data, ask for it to be corrected, ask for it to be erased where no other basis requires it to be kept, object to processing based on legitimate interest, and withdraw consent to marketing at any time.

Requests are forwarded to the privacy mailbox on the day they are received, by whoever receives them, in whatever form they arrive. A request does not have to mention this policy, the law, or the word request to count. The statutory response period is one month from receipt by the company, extendable by two further months for complex requests where the individual is told of the extension within the first month. Legal verifies the identity of the requester before disclosing anything, and no one else responds to such a request directly.

## Sharing, transfers and processors

Personal data is shared outside the company only where there is a lawful basis and, where the recipient processes on the company's behalf, a written processing agreement in place before the first transfer. The agreement sets out the purpose, the duration, the security measures and the position on sub-processors.

Transfers outside the European Economic Area require an approved mechanism, which in practice means standard contractual clauses with a documented transfer risk assessment. Legal maintains the list of approved recipients and mechanisms. Nobody may sign up to a new third-party service that will process personal data without Legal and IT Security reviewing it first, and this includes free services and trials, which are the most common route by which personal data leaves a company unnoticed.

## Breaches and accountability

A personal data breach is any event leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to personal data. It is reported through the security incident process immediately, and the seventy-two hour notification clock starts when any part of the company becomes aware, not when Legal does.

Legal assesses whether the breach must be notified to a supervisory authority and whether the affected individuals must be told directly. The assessment and its reasoning are documented whether or not a notification follows, because the obligation to document a decision not to notify is as real as the obligation to notify. The data protection officer reports annually to the board on the processing register, the breach log, requests received, and the state of retention compliance across the systems in scope.
