## Scope and thresholds

This procedure covers the purchase of goods and services by the company, from stationery to subcontracted linehaul. It does not cover the employment of people, the acquisition of businesses, or the purchase of land and buildings, each of which has its own approval route.

Purchases below 2,000 EUR per year with a single supplier may be made directly by a budget holder against an approved cost centre. Between 2,000 and 25,000 EUR, three written quotations are obtained and recorded. Above 25,000 EUR, a formal tender is run. Above 250,000 EUR, or for any multi-year commitment, the tender outcome goes to the executive committee for approval before an award is communicated. Splitting a requirement to fall below a threshold is treated as a policy breach, not as good planning.

## Vendor onboarding checks

Before a first purchase order is raised, a supplier is onboarded. Onboarding records the legal entity name, registration number, registered address, VAT number, bank details and beneficial ownership where the entity is not listed.

Procurement screens the supplier and its beneficial owners against sanctions lists and adverse media, and re-screens annually and on any change of ownership. Financial standing is checked through a credit report for any supplier expected to exceed 50,000 EUR per year. Insurance certificates are collected where the service creates liability exposure, and their expiry dates are recorded so renewals are chased rather than discovered. Suppliers handling personal data on the company's behalf are not onboarded until Legal has a processing agreement in place, and suppliers with access to the company systems are not onboarded until IT Security has reviewed them.

## Bank details and payment fraud controls

Bank details are collected at onboarding on the supplier's letterhead or through the supplier portal, never from an email body. They are verified by telephone to a number obtained independently of the document supplying the details, and the verification is recorded with the date, the number called and the name of the person spoken to.

A change to existing bank details follows exactly the same verification, without exception, regardless of how routine the request appears or how senior the person asking. Two people in Finance approve any change before it takes effect, and the first payment after a change is held for twenty-four hours. Urgency in a request to change bank details is itself a warning sign rather than a reason to shorten the process, and staff are explicitly authorised to be slow about it.

## Running a tender

A tender begins with a written specification of what is required, the evaluation criteria and their weightings, agreed before any supplier sees the documents. Criteria typically cover capability, service level, price, financial stability, sustainability and risk, and price is rarely weighted above fifty percent.

All bidders receive the same information at the same time, and any clarification given to one is circulated to all. Bids are received to a single mailbox and not opened before the deadline. Evaluation is carried out by at least two people scoring independently against the agreed criteria, and scores are reconciled in a recorded meeting. Unsuccessful bidders are told promptly and offered a debrief. The evaluation record is retained for seven years, because a tender that cannot be explained two years later is indistinguishable from one that was not run properly.

## Conflicts of interest and gifts

Anyone involved in a purchasing decision declares any personal interest in a bidder before the evaluation begins: a family relationship, a financial interest, a former employment within the past two years, or any other connection a reasonable observer might think relevant.

A declared interest does not automatically exclude someone, but the decision on whether it does belongs to the procurement lead and not to the individual. An undeclared interest discovered afterwards invalidates the award and is treated as a conduct matter. Gifts and hospitality from bidders are declined outright during a live tender, in any amount. Outside a tender, the limits in the travel and expense policy apply, and anything offered above them is declined and reported to Compliance.

## Contracting and signature authority

Awards are documented in a written contract before work begins. Purchase orders are not contracts and do not incorporate the company's terms on their own. Where a supplier's standard terms are used, Legal reviews them for liability caps, indemnities, termination rights, data protection and governing law before signature.

Signature authority follows the delegation schedule: budget holders sign to 25,000 EUR, function heads to 250,000 EUR, and the managing director above that, with the executive committee approval recorded. Nobody signs a contract that commits the company beyond their own authority, and nobody signs on behalf of someone else. Work started before a signed contract is a common source of dispute, and Procurement will not retrospectively paper an arrangement without the function head acknowledging in writing why it happened.

## Managing the supplier after award

Every supplier above 50,000 EUR per year has a named owner inside the company, recorded in the contract register, responsible for performance and for the relationship. Contracts, renewal dates, notice periods and price review dates are held centrally, and owners are reminded ninety days before a renewal or notice deadline.

Performance is reviewed against the contracted service levels at a frequency set in the contract, typically quarterly for strategic suppliers. Reviews are documented, including where performance is satisfactory, so that a pattern is visible over time. Auto-renewals are the most expensive form of inattention in procurement: a contract that renews because nobody diarised the notice date costs the company the difference between the incumbent price and the market for the whole of the next term.
